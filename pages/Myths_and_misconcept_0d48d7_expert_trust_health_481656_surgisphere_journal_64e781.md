## Introduction

The Surgisphere episode became one of the clearest demonstrations of how a prestigious journal can briefly lend enormous authority to a health claim before the underlying evidence has been adequately tested. During the first months of the COVID-19 pandemic, studies based on a database supplied by the company Surgisphere appeared in two of the world's most respected medical journals, *The Lancet* and *The New England Journal of Medicine* (NEJM). The papers influenced clinical research, public debate and policy decisions before concerns about the underlying data led to retractions. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2931324-6/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelancet.com">[The Lancet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelancet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The LancetRetraction—Hydroxychloroquine or chloroquine with or...by MR Mehra · 2020 · Cited by 1965 — RETRACTED: Hydroxychloroquine or c...</span></span></span>

For readers trying to understand [myths and misconceptions]({{ 'myths-and-misconceptions/' | relative_url }}) in health, the lesson is not that journals are untrustworthy. ## How urgent publication raised the stakes

In May 2020, as governments and researchers urgently searched for COVID-19 treatments, *The Lancet* published a study claiming that hydroxychloroquine and related drug combinations were associated with increased mortality and heart complications in hospitalised COVID-19 patients. The analysis was said to draw on a massive international database covering tens of thousands of patients from hundreds of hospitals worldwide. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2931324-6/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelancet.com">[The Lancet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelancet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The LancetRetraction—Hydroxychloroquine or chloroquine with or...by MR Mehra · 2020 · Cited by 1965 — RETRACTED: Hydroxychloroquine or c...</span></span></span>

The [timing]({{ 'timing/' | relative_url }}) mattered. The world was desperate for evidence, and hydroxychloroquine had become one of the most controversial proposed COVID-19 treatments. Because the paper appeared in a leading journal and appeared to use an enormous dataset, it carried unusual influence. News organisations reported the findings widely, and international research efforts responded rapidly. The World Health Organization temporarily paused the hydroxychloroquine arm of its Solidarity trial while safety concerns were examined. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/jun/03/covid-19-surgisphere-who-world-health-organization-hydroxychloroquine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">covid 19 surgisphere who world health organization hydroxychloroquine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianSurgisphere: governments and WHO changed Covid-19...3 Jun 2020 — On Wednesday, the NEJM and the Lancet published an expressi...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/jun/03/covid-19-surgisphere-who-world-health-organization-hydroxychloroquine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">covid 19 surgisphere who world health organization hydroxychloroquine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianSurgisphere: governments and WHO changed Covid-19...3 Jun 2020 — On Wednesday, the NEJM and the Lancet published an expressi...</span></span></span>

This is the danger of rushed journal authority. Under normal circumstances, scientific claims are tested over time through replication, criticism and competing analyses. During a public-health emergency, however, publication itself can trigger immediate action. The prestige of the journal becomes a shortcut for trust, and decisions may be made before the broader scientific community has had time to examine the evidence in depth. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2931324-6/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelancet.com">[The Lancet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelancet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The LancetRetraction—Hydroxychloroquine or chloroquine with or...by MR Mehra · 2020 · Cited by 1965 — RETRACTED: Hydroxychloroquine or c...</span></span></span>

The problem was not merely that the findings later proved unreliable. It was that the institutional authority attached to publication magnified their impact before verification had caught up.

## Why peer review was not enough on its own

The Surgisphere studies are often cited as evidence that peer review failed. The reality is more specific and more instructive.

Peer review is designed to evaluate whether a paper's methods, analysis and conclusions appear reasonable based on the information available to reviewers. It is generally not a forensic audit of every underlying data point. In the Surgisphere case, the crucial weakness involved access to the underlying database. As outside researchers began examining the published papers, they noticed inconsistencies and questioned whether the company could realistically have assembled such a vast multinational dataset in the manner claimed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/search/research-news/11590" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NCBINews: The Surgisphere Scandal: What Went</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Scientist)Scientists have raised questions about the dataset published in The Lancet last week that triggered the suspension of clin...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tctmd.com/news/covid-19-upset-researchers-retract-lancet-nejm-papers-hydroxychloroquine-aceiarbs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tctmd.com">[tctmd.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tctmd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COVID-19 Upset: Researchers Retract Lancet, NEJM...Jun 4, 2020 — The reason stems from an inability to access the raw data to validate t...</span></span></span>

The concerns escalated rapidly. Independent reviewers sought to audit the data, but Surgisphere did not provide the full dataset needed for verification. Several authors eventually stated that they could no longer vouch for the primary data and requested retraction. Both *The Lancet* and NEJM withdrew the papers after it became clear that an independent audit could not be completed. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2931324-6/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelancet.com">[The Lancet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelancet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The LancetRetraction—Hydroxychloroquine or chloroquine with or...by MR Mehra · 2020 · Cited by 1965 — RETRACTED: Hydroxychloroquine or c...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc2021225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nejm.org">[New England]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nejm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Disclosure forms provided by the authors are available with the full text of this letter at NEJM.org.Read mo...</span></span></span> Journal of Medicine

A common misconception is that publication means a study has been exhaustively checked by journals. The Surgisphere episode showed the limits of that assumption:

* Peer reviewers usually assess evidence presented by authors rather than independently recreating large proprietary databases.
* Extraordinary datasets can appear plausible on paper even when important questions remain unanswered.
* Journal prestige cannot substitute for transparency and independent verification.
* During emergencies, accelerated publication schedules may reduce the time available for broader scrutiny. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/932262" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medscape.com">[Medscape]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medscape.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Did This Pass Peer Review?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The COVID-19 Retractions15 Jun 2020 — Furthermore, the two retracted Surgisphere papers did not seem to include any such impossibilities...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7436724/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PMCPublishing coronavirology: Peering into peer(less?) reviewby T Pederson · 2020 · Cited by 4 — The Lancet study was observational, not...</span></span></span>

The key lesson is not that peer review is useless. It is that peer review is one layer of quality control within a larger system that also depends on openness, replication and post-publication criticism.

## How retractions can repair trust or feed suspicion

Once concerns became impossible to ignore, the journals issued expressions of concern and then retractions. From a scientific perspective, this was the system correcting itself. Retractions exist precisely because published research is not assumed to be infallible. ## What the Surgisphere case teaches about authority

The lasting importance of Surgisphere lies less in the specific hydroxychloroquine debate and more in what it revealed about evidence and authority.

Many people assume a hierarchy in which a published paper automatically becomes established fact, and a paper in a prestigious journal becomes nearly unquestionable fact. The Surgisphere case showed that this shortcut can be dangerous. A claim should be judged not only by where it appears but also by whether the underlying evidence is transparent, reproducible and open to independent verification. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2931324-6/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelancet.com">[The Lancet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelancet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The LancetRetraction—Hydroxychloroquine or chloroquine with or...by MR Mehra · 2020 · Cited by 1965 — RETRACTED: Hydroxychloroquine or c...</span></span></span>

For doctors, journalists and the public, the practical lesson is straightforward: respect journal authority, but do not confuse it with final authority.
